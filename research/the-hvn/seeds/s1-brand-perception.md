# Briefing S1: How is The HVN perceived? (Brand, trust and luxury)

Briefing for a simulated online discussion. It describes a real London clinic, The HVN, using only public information checked on 15 October 2026. It contains no customer or patient data. Staff are referred to by role. The twelve discussion participants in Part D are invented archetypes, not real people.

Reliability labels used below:
- [Published] stated on The HVN's own website, loaded live on 15 October 2026.
- [Reported] stated by press, trade media, directories or regulators; may be out of date.
- [Hypothesis] an analyst's inference, not a fact.
- [Invented] a fictional discussion participant.

## Part A: The HVN, facts

The HVN is a clinic at 57–63 Knightsbridge, London SW1X 7BF, trading as The HVN Wellbeing Ltd [Published]. It opened in October 2023 [Reported]. Opening hours are Monday to Saturday 08:30–21:30 and Sunday 10:00–18:00, and enquiries go to a "Concierge Team" [Published].

How The HVN describes itself [Published]:
- Every page carries the line "London's leading Wellness & Longevity Clinic".
- About page: "wellness is not just a goal—it's an ongoing journey"; "clinical innovation … balanced with unbridled attention to care through our bespoke treatment plans"; "state of the art facilities, comprehensive laboratory assessments and transformational treatments".
- Every client journey "begins with a personalised consultation", which may lead to testing, a bespoke programme or a referral within the team.
- The website's own page titles describe it in different ways: "Wellness & Longevity Clinic London | Acupuncture" (home page), "Integrative Wellness & Longevity Clinic" (About), "Expert Skin & Hair Clinic Support" (Contact) and "Book Your Skin & Hair Treatment Appointment" (Booking).
- Earlier press coverage described it as "the home of conscious wellbeing" and "not a spa" but a spa–clinic hybrid [Reported, 2023–2024]. That "conscious wellbeing" wording no longer appears on its main pages [Published, checked 15 Oct 2026].

Leadership and team [Published / Reported]:
- Founded by a former chief executive of a global fashion and fragrance house [Reported].
- Medical Director: a GP-trained doctor (MRCGP) described as a global award-winning laser specialist [Published].
- A second aesthetic doctor with surgical and GP experience who also covers menopause and women's health [Published].
- A senior Traditional Chinese Medicine practitioner and acupuncturist with a named signature method [Published].
- An osteopath who also runs a £750 "HRU Health & Performance Assessment" measuring 230 physiological markers [Published].
- Aesthetic nurse, aesthetic practitioner, holistic and massage therapists [Published].

What it offers [Published]. Five service families on one site:
1. Face and skin aesthetics: Fotona laser lifts, muscle-relaxing injections (from £260), dermal fillers, Profhilo, polynucleotides, exosomes, thread lifts, peels and facials. Aesthetic consultation £175 for 60 minutes.
2. Traditional Chinese Medicine: acupuncture (from £200) for pain, fertility and IVF, menopause, hormonal health, stress and skin; cupping; acupressure.
3. Wellness and recovery: the Hydrobed Reset Massage (£250 for 60 minutes; the clinic calls the Hydrobed a "dry flotation & holistic massage" experience, and 2024 press described it as the only one in the UK), reflexology, rituals, osteopathy, oxygen–ozone therapy.
4. IV and injections: 14 IV infusions priced £275–£425 for 60–90 minutes; NAD+ infusions in tiers at £350, £500 and £800; vitamin injections.
5. Testing and longevity: blood profiles at £450 (50+ biomarkers) and £550 (60+), with a £150 doctor follow-up call; Discovery Consultation £350 (up to 60 minutes; £100 redeemable against functional medicine packages); 8R Longevity Programme with partner clinic Levitas "from £3,000"; 8R consultation £400.

Things not currently published [Published, checked 15 Oct 2026]:
- Programme prices other than 8R "from £3,000". Earlier fixed programmes (for example a £14,500 16-week "360°" programme and an £8,000 sleep programme) no longer have live pages.
- The functional medicine programmes page has no prices.
- No standalone hyperbaric oxygen page, although press in 2024 reported a medical-grade hyperbaric chamber [Reported].

Trust signals:
- A "CQC Regulated" badge appears on every page [Published]. The Care Quality Commission register listed the provider as registered but **not yet inspected**, so no rating, when last checked in October 2026 [Reported].
- A Google-reviews widget is embedded on most pages [Published]; the overall rating was not visible.
- Most pages link to press coverage in Forbes, Condé Nast Traveller, Hello!, Elite Traveller, City A.M. and Quintessentially [Published].
- One June 2025 personal blog post described a skin-analysis result that a dermatologist later disputed, and the writer felt products were being sold to her [Reported, single anecdote].
- Two different phone numbers appear on different pages [Published].
- Cancellation needs 24 hours' notice; appointments need a card on file [Published].
- IV pages describe benefits such as helping the body "fight off infections" and supporting "cellular repair … and healthy ageing" [Published]. The UK advertising regulator (ASA) has previously ruled against other IV-drip providers for unsupported health claims [Reported]. [Hypothesis] Evidence-literate readers may see a tension between these claims and a "clinical" positioning. This is not a legal finding.
