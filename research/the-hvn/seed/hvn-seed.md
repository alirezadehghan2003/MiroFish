# Briefing: The HVN and the London Luxury Wellness and Longevity Market

This briefing describes a real London clinic, The HVN, and its competitive environment, using only publicly available information gathered in October 2026. It contains no customer or patient data. People are referred to by role, not name.

Every statement carries a reliability label:
- **[Published]**: stated on The HVN's own website or a competitor's own website, as indexed by a search engine. It may be out of date.
- **[Reported]**: stated by independent press, directories or regulators.
- **[Hypothesis]**: an analyst's inference, not established fact.
- **[Hypothetical segment]**: an invented audience archetype used for simulation, not a real customer.

Prices are as published and may have changed.

---

## 1. The HVN: who they are

The HVN is a wellness, longevity and medical aesthetics clinic at 57–63 Knightsbridge, London SW1X 7BF. It opened in October 2023 [Reported].

**Leadership** [Reported]:
- The founder and CEO previously led Paco Rabanne, a global fashion and fragrance house.
- A Chief Operating Officer with a beauty-industry background.
- A Medical Director, described on The HVN's website as a GMC-registered doctor specialising in laser and aesthetic medicine with 30 years of clinical experience [Published].

**Clinical team** [Published]: includes an aesthetic doctor, a Traditional Chinese Medicine lead, and an osteopath who specialises in pain.

**Regulation** [Reported]: The HVN is registered with the Care Quality Commission (CQC), England's health and social care regulator, as "The HVN Wellbeing Ltd". The CQC lists the service as **not yet inspected**, so it has no CQC rating. The website footer displays a "CQC Regulated" label [Published].

**Reviews:** no independent customer reviews of The HVN were found on Google, Trustpilot or Treatwell in search results [Reported]. This may mean reviews exist but were not indexed.

## 2. The HVN's positioning and brand language

The HVN's website describes the clinic as **"the home of conscious wellbeing"**: a tailored, evidence-based approach to wellness through personalised plans, rooted in "authenticity, transparency, and conscious luxury" [Published].

- **Homepage title:** "The HVN: Wellness & Longevity Clinic London" [Published].
- **Longevity promise:** helping people "feel better for longer", with a philosophy of "science and sensibility". The aim is to extend the years lived at peak performance, physically, mentally and emotionally [Published].
- **Method:** combining advanced technologies with ancient practices and functional medicine to build bespoke, root-cause treatment plans [Published].

Press coverage describes The HVN as "not a spa" but a hybrid of spa and clinic [Reported].

Signature facilities reported by press [Reported]:
- a **Hydrobed**, a massage bed using warm pressurised water, described as the only one in the UK (unverified claim);
- **Fotona** laser technology;
- a medical-grade **hyperbaric oxygen chamber**;
- a calming space inspired by forest bathing.

**Partnership** [Published]: The HVN delivers the **8R Longevity Programme** with **Levitas**, a London longevity clinic. The programme was developed by Levitas's lead clinician.

**Analyst view** [Hypothesis]:
- The HVN combines five categories under one roof: spa rituals, medical aesthetics, functional medicine, IV and hyperbaric "biohacking", and longevity programmes.
- That breadth is distinctive, but first-time visitors may be unsure whether it is a spa, a medical clinic or a longevity centre.
- Its luxury codes come more from fashion and beauty (founder pedigree, "conscious luxury", concierge service) than from medical heritage.

## 3. The HVN's services and published prices

The HVN publishes prices on most service pages [Published]. Single treatments and consultations:

- **Aesthetic consultation:** £175 for 60 minutes.
- **Functional Medicine Discovery Session:** £100, credited towards a programme.
- **8R Longevity Consultation:** £400, up to 90 minutes. Covers biological-age testing options such as GlycanAge and DNA methylation tests.
- **IV infusions:** £275 to £425 for 60–90 minutes. NAD+ infusions from £350. An iron infusion delivered with a partner provider from £895.
- **Anti-Jetlag Programme** (includes hyperbaric oxygen): £375 for 90 minutes. No standalone hyperbaric price was found.
- **Muscle-relaxing injections:** from £260. **Dermal fillers:** from £450. **Mesotherapy:** from £500.
- **HydrO2 facial:** from £200. **Acupuncture:** from £200 (course from £1,620). **Osteopathy:** from £260.
- **Hydrobed Reset Massage:** £250 for 60 minutes [Reported by a travel review].

Programmes [Published]:

- **Baseline Wellness:** £999 over 4 weeks.
- **Signature Wellness:** £3,500 over 3 months.
- **8R Longevity Programme** (with Levitas): from £3,000.
- **Sleep, Rest, Recover, De-Stress Programme:** £8,000 over 10 weeks.
- **Skin Prevent, Rejuvenate & Restore Programme:** £12,000 over 12 weeks.
- **360° Proactive Health Programme:** £14,500 over 16 weeks. Includes body and skin analysis, vital signs, functional medicine testing, nutrition and meal planning, supplements, IV therapy and hyperbaric oxygen.
- **Annual Longevity Programme:** 12 months with quarterly advanced testing and monthly one-to-one support. **No price is published.**

**Booking terms** [Published, terms page possibly out of date]:
- A payment card must be stored to confirm any appointment.
- Cancellations need 24 hours' notice. Late cancellations and no-shows are charged 100% of the service price.
- Cancellations are accepted only by phone or through the booking platform.

**Opening hours** [Published]: Monday to Saturday 08:30–21:30, Sunday 10:00–18:00.

**Analyst view** [Hypothesis]:
- Publishing prices is unusual at this level and can build trust.
- However, there are few options between single treatments (around £200–£500) and programmes (£999–£14,500), and the annual programme has no published price. This may make the step from first visit to programme feel like a big commitment.

## 4. Website and trust signals

Observations from indexed website pages [Published]:
- Two different phone numbers appear across the site.
- Many programme pages exist under two different web addresses, which suggests an unfinished website migration.
- A page address for a Fotona laser treatment says "5D" while its title says "4D".

**Advertising regulation** [Reported]:
- The UK Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) has ruled against other London IV-drip providers for claiming health benefits without adequate evidence in healthy people, and against advertising prescription-only injections to the public.
- The HVN's IV pages describe benefits such as boosting energy, anti-ageing support, cellular repair, and faster recovery from colds, flu or infections [Published].

**Analyst view** [Hypothesis]: these claims sit in tension with The HVN's "evidence-based" positioning and may concern evidence-literate buyers. This is not a legal finding.

**Aesthetics regulation** [Reported]: England plans a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures such as injections and fillers. It was **not yet in force** as of 2026.

## 5. Competitors

Published positioning and prices, as found online. Several sources conflict or may be outdated.

- **Lanserhof at The Arts Club (Mayfair).**
  - A medical-led members' gym and health club [Reported].
  - Membership reported at about £3,200 a year plus a £1,600 assessment fee in a recent guide, but older sources give £6,500 a year plus a £1,500 joining fee. **The figures conflict.**
  - A 2022 price list shows an integrative consultation at £390 and a functional medicine consultation at £550 [Published, 2022].
- **HUM2N (Chelsea).** A physician-led longevity and "biohacking" clinic [Published].
  - NAD+ IV infusions £295–£450.
  - Hyperbaric oxygen from £180 per session, or about £990–£1,170 for five sessions.
  - Longevity programmes priced as **monthly memberships**: £495 a month plus £995 one-off, or £695 a month plus £1,495 one-off.
  - A two-hour combined protocol at £795 (promotional).
- **The Wellness Clinic at Harrods (Knightsbridge).** A medical aesthetics and wellness floor inside Harrods department store, hosting independent practitioners and brands, including IV-therapy concessions [Published].
  - Facial consultation £195. Profhilo from £800. Polynucleotide treatments £1,500–£2,500.
  - Some programmes are "price on request"; a 50% deposit is typical.
  - Some price pages are several years old.
- **The Lanesborough Club & Spa (Knightsbridge).** A luxury hotel members' club and spa with an advanced aesthetics, regenerative and longevity offering led by a partner doctor [Published].
  - Regenerative facial £500. Aesthetics day packages £400–£1,000.
  - Membership reported at about £3,500 a year in a recent guide, but £6,600 a year plus a £2,000 joining fee in older press. **The figures conflict.**
- **IV Drip Clinic London (Mayfair).** A CQC-registered IV and longevity clinic. Reported prices: NAD+ from £350, vitamin drips from £260, hyperbaric oxygen from £145 for 30 minutes [Reported by a directory].
- **The Online GP (Marylebone).** A diagnostics-led longevity testing provider [Published].
  - Longevity blood panel from £645; epigenetic biological-age test from £595; 100-plus biomarker assessment from £1,400.
  - Continuous care from £24,995 a year.

**Analyst view** [Hypothesis]:
- Most of The HVN's individual treatments (IV, hyperbaric oxygen, injectables, lasers) are also available from these competitors at broadly similar published prices.
- The HVN's likely advantage is the combination of services, setting and service under one roof, rather than any single treatment.
- Competitors such as HUM2N and Lanserhof sell memberships, while The HVN sells fixed-term programmes.

## 6. Market context

The Global Wellness Institute values the UK wellness economy at about US$224 billion in 2022, rising to about US$261 billion in 2024, fifth largest in the world [Reported]. This covers the whole UK wellness economy, not the London luxury clinic segment. **No reliable figure for the London luxury longevity and aesthetics segment was found.**

## 7. Hypothetical audience segments (for simulation only)

These six segments are invented archetypes, inferred from The HVN's services, prices and location. They are **not real customers** and are not based on customer data.

1. **The Burnt-Out Executive** [Hypothetical segment]
   - A senior professional in their 40s or 50s, living or working in central London, struggling with fatigue, poor sleep and frequent travel.
   - Values speed, discretion, medical credibility and evening or weekend appointments.
   - Sceptical about whether IV drips and hyperbaric oxygen are evidence-based. Hesitant about £8,000–£14,500 programmes and a 10–16 week commitment.
2. **The Longevity Optimiser** [Hypothetical segment]
   - A data-driven 35–60-year-old who follows longevity science and wants biological-age testing and measurable results.
   - Compares The HVN with Lanserhof, HUM2N and diagnostics-only providers.
   - Sceptical of spa elements; wants full test lists and prices.
3. **The Aesthetics-First Client** [Hypothetical segment]
   - Someone in their 30s to 50s focused on skin quality, injectables and laser treatments.
   - Values practitioner skill, natural results and safety.
   - Has many Knightsbridge alternatives, including Harrods, and weighs consultation fees and per-treatment prices.
4. **The International Visitor** [Hypothetical segment]
   - A wealthy overseas client visiting London for a short stay, interested in jet-lag recovery and treatments "while in London".
   - Values prestige, location and concierge service.
   - Concerned about short-notice availability and the 100% late-cancellation charge.
5. **The Holistic Wellbeing Seeker** [Hypothetical segment]
   - Someone drawn to acupuncture, Traditional Chinese Medicine and gut and hormone health.
   - Values warmth, holistic philosophy and continuity with the same practitioner.
   - May find a clinical setting cold and course prices high.
6. **The Gatekeeper** [Hypothetical segment]
   - A spouse, personal assistant or family-office manager who researches and books on behalf of a wealthy client.
   - Focuses on regulation, clear pricing, cancellation terms and reputation.
   - Notes the lack of a CQC inspection rating and of independent reviews.
